To knock out the engine question first: if you want a seamless mobile Chrome PWA but want a real architecture behind it, look at Godot 4. It exports native HTML5/WebAssembly that runs perfectly as a PWA on mobile browsers. There are multiple open-source, MIT-licensed vehicle physics and 3D mech controller repos on GitHub (like the Godot VehicleBody3D starter kits) that you can clone today and use as the immediate baseline. It keeps you out of the weeds of raw engine building so you can focus entirely on the mechanics.
The concept itself is absolute heat. A three-stance combat racing system changes the entire flow of a derby or a race because you aren't just managing speed and weapons—you're managing physical state.
Here is how you make those three modes actually matter so players don't just default to one:
 * Full Vehicle Mode (Momentum & Evasion): Pure speed and physics. This is your Twisted Metal state. You use this to close gaps, hit ramps, run away, or execute high-speed ramming attacks. The handling is entirely drift and momentum-based.
 * Hybrid Mode (The Gun Platform): Think of a car popping its front half up into a torso, or wheels shifting into hover-pods. You lose top speed, but you gain 360-degree aiming and omnidirectional strafing. This is your mid-range DPS state. You're circle-strafing targets, locking on with missiles, and holding down choke points.
 * Full Robot Mode (The Brawler): Slowest movement, highest defense, brutal close-range output. This is where the WWE Crush Hour DNA kicks in. If someone gets too close in a derby, you shift to full mech, grab their vehicle, and physically suplex them into a wall or rip their armor off. It’s for holding ground, tanking hits, and heavy melee.
The gameplay loop writes itself. Imagine hitting a massive ramp in a circuit race as a car, transforming into full mech mid-air to hit a dropping elbow smash on the guy in first place, and then immediately snapping into hybrid mode upon landing to strafe the rest of the pack with machine-gun fire.
It completely rewrites the rules of a demolition derby. You aren't just trading paint—you're deciding if you want to outrun them, out-shoot them, or stand up and fight them bare-handed.
